Overview

Targeting TRPV1
in heart failure

Makai develops TRPV1 antagonists for cardiovascular disease. In preclinical pressure-overload models, TRPV1 contributes to cardiac hypertrophy and heart-failure progression, and orally delivered TRPV1 antagonists reverse already-established cardiac dysfunction in mice. The work rests on target-biology and method-of-use intellectual property exclusively licensed from the University of Hawaii.

TRPV1 is a physical component of the cardiac natriuretic-peptide receptor (NPR1 / cGMP / PKG) signalling complex. In mouse pressure-overload models, orally delivered TRPV1 antagonists reduce atrial natriuretic peptide and reverse already-established cardiac dysfunction.

Horton et al., Channels, 2019
The Science

TRPV1 Ion Channel Discovery

Twenty years of research revealed that TRPV1—best known for detecting chili pepper heat—drives heart failure progression.

The Discovery

Heart failure affects nearly 6.7 million US adults, and current drugs slow its progression rather than reverse it. TRPV1—the channel best known for sensing chili-pepper heat—is also expressed in cardiac tissue, where it contributes to pressure-overload hypertrophy and heart-failure progression. Removing TRPV1 function, genetically or pharmacologically, is protective in preclinical models.

  • Every oral/systemic TRPV1 antagonist tested — BCTC, SB366791 and AMG9810, three structurally distinct molecules — reversed the early loss of cardiac function after pressure overload; the effect tracks the target, not one compound.
  • Oral BCTC, started after loss of function was established, reversed fractional shortening within weeks and held function above vehicle to 32 weeks.
  • With treatment, less of what drives failure: heart-weight gain, cardiomyocyte hypertrophy, fibrosis, ANP, inflammation and tissue remodelling.
  • Healthier animals — body weight significantly higher through 32 weeks (P<0.0001).
  • TRPV1-null mice are protected from pressure-overload hypertrophy.
  • The TRPV1–NPR1 interaction and PKG phosphorylation of TRPV1 are demonstrated biochemically; the broader cardiac mechanism remains a proposed model.
  • Independent human signal: a Pila Pharma Phase 2 trial of a TRPV1 antagonist reported reduced ANP.

Mechanism

Proposed mechanism: TRPV1 in the natriuretic-peptide signalling complex

TRPV1 physically associates with the natriuretic-peptide receptor NPR1. In the proposed model, ANP → NPR1/GC → cGMP → PKG phosphorylates and inhibits TRPV1. The interaction and the phosphorylation are shown biochemically; the cardiac cell type and the precise site of action are not yet defined. What is clear functionally is the outcome: blocking TRPV1 — pharmacologically or genetically — reduces calcium entry and is protective in these models.

Clinical context — illustrative

What the preserved function could mean in the clinic

  • A class effect, not one drug. The aortic band is never removed — permanent pressure overload — yet all three TRPV1 antagonists (BCTC, SB366791, AMG9810) reversed the early loss of function. The arm followed longest held function above vehicle to 32 weeks.
  • The benefit. At 3 weeks post-banding (2 weeks post-treatment), every drug produced a benefit of ~8–11 EF points over vehicle (AMG9810 ~8, a trend; SB366791 ~10; BCTC ~11). Only BCTC was followed to 32 weeks, sustaining ~7 points — the conservative basis for the estimate below.
  • Illustrative risk translation. With the human EF–outcome relationships — +39% mortality hazard per 10-point EF fall <45% (Solomon 2005) and ~2× cardiac-event risk per 10-point fall (Dakik 1996) — that maps to a reduction in all-cause mortality hazard of ~20–30% and in cardiac-event risk of ~35–45%.
  • A conservative floor. The mouse is banded for life — the overload is never relieved — so these are worst-case numbers. In patients, where the underlying overload can itself be treated, the benefit could be greater. (The EF relationships apply below 45%, outside the mouse range, so treat the figures as directional.)
